# Comparative Literature Survey (20 Research Papers)

This document contains a systematic comparative analysis of twenty (20) verified academic research papers across the foundational domains of **Enhanced SW**: Document OCR & Receipt Parsing, Financial Text Classification & Expense Categorization, Time-Series Spending Forecasting, Financial Anomaly Detection, Combinatorial Debt Settlement, and Multi-Provider LLM Structured Extraction.

The 20 papers are structured into six coherent thematic pillars:
1. **Receipt OCR & Document Layout Extraction** (Papers 1–5)

3. **Graph-Theoretic Debt Simplification & Settlement** (Papers 10–12)
4. **Statistical & Deep Anomaly Detection in Personal Finance** (Papers 13–16)
5. **Short-Text Transaction & Expense Categorization** (Papers 17–19)
6. **Tool Augmentation & Structured LLM Reasoning** (Paper 20)

## 3. Systematic Identification of the Research Gap

A critical synthesis of the 20 surveyed works reveals three fundamental structural limitations in existing academic and commercial systems:

1. **The Disconnect Between Semantic Extraction and Deterministic Arithmetic**: Existing Document AI approaches (Huang et al., 2022; Du et al., 2020) and LLM extraction pipelines frequently suffer from mathematical hallucinations when attempting to calculate subtotals, proportional taxes, discounts, or individual shares directly in language generation. Conversely, deterministic systems lack the semantic flexibility to parse unstructured natural language consumption instructions (e.g., *"Rahul had the pizza and shared fries with Sneha"*).
2. **Lack of Resilient Multi-Provider Orchestration**: Most existing financial NLP applications rely on single-provider API models (Liu & Zhang, 2021), rendering them brittle to rate limits, token quotas, and regional API outages.
3. **Absence of Unified End-to-End Personal Financial Management Workflows**: Current literature addresses receipt OCR, spending forecasting, anomaly detection, and debt settlement in isolated silos. Commercial tools (e.g., Splitwise, Mint) either lack receipt-level natural language splitting or fail to integrate predictive forecasting (Prophet) and explainable leave-one-out statistical anomaly detection into an offline-resilient architecture.

**Enhanced SW bridges this research gap** by implementing a decoupled, fault-tolerant microservice architecture where:
- Local lightweight OCR (PP-OCR) and multi-provider LLMs (Gemini $\to$ Mistral $\to$ Groq) handle unstructured semantic comprehension.
- Deterministic Python `Decimal` engines guarantee exact arithmetic, penny reconciliation, and $O(N)$ greedy debt simplification.
- Meta Prophet and leave-one-out statistical models deliver transparent, explainable forecasting and anomaly detection.
